# WATCHTOWER AI | Two revenue tracks to test
**Status:** Hypotheses only. No buyer demand, pricing acceptance, signed deals, or recurring revenue has been proven.

## Track A: Execute carefully selected opportunities
**Customer pays for:** a fixed-scope digital deliverable completed under MLive responsibility, potentially by an independently qualified subcontractor.

**Sequence:** Original demand → buyer verified → defined scope → supplier search → direct vendor quote + due diligence → payment milestones → risk & compliance gates → owner-approved proposal → signed agreement → supervised delivery → cash collected and costs paid.

**Illustrative example (not a forecast):**
- Customer project $5,000
- Subcontractor $3,000; other direct costs $250; contingency $500
- Forecast contribution $1,250 (25%) **before overhead, tax and founder compensation**
- Vendor upfront $350, other upfront $100, contingency $500 = conservative $950 exposure
- Client deposit is not used to erase refundable liabilities or the conservative capital guardrail

**Risks:** project management, contractor unreliability, rework, refunds, nonpayment, compliance, cash overlap and time burden.

**Evaluation:** paid contribution per owner hour; actual working capital at risk; project margin vs quoted margin; customer acceptance; repeatability.

## Track B: Sell bounded opportunity intelligence
**Customer pays for:** research, source verification and useful summaries rather than delivery of the underlying project.

**Candidate first pilot (not a commitment):** Four weekly research briefs, each up to three relevant, current, original-source opportunity leads, with buyer/source links, scope fit, deadline and unknowns clearly labeled. No guaranteed awards, vendor commitments, proposal submissions or financial returns.

**Initial pricing experiment:** Ask about a *hypothetical* monthly fee range, then require an actual accepted paid pilot to validate willingness to pay. Do not claim a subscription business from a verbal compliment.

**Illustrative target math (not a forecast):** Three paying clients at $1,000/month = $3,000 revenue. If combined direct fulfillment costs were $500, contribution would be $2,500 **before overhead, taxes, sales time, founder compensation and refunds**. Both the price and the $500 cost assumption must be validated through actual customer experience.

**Risks:** sourcing and verification labor, limited exclusivity of public notices, freshness, refund/service-level obligations, retention, error liability, and difficulty finding buyers willing to pay for publicly accessible information.

**Delivery promises to avoid:** exclusive government access, guaranteed contract wins, prequalified subcontractors, compliant legal eligibility certificates, or anything resembling regulated government award success fees without expert review.

**Evaluation:** paid pilots / approached prospects; verified opportunities delivered per brief; research hours per client; renewals and retention; realized contribution after delivery costs; complaint/error rate; source licensing compliance.

## Side-by-side test
| Measure | Outsourced deal | Research service |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer willingness to pay | Signed scope and payment | Paid pilot, not verbal enthusiasm |
| Main scarce input | Qualified delivery partner and client trust | Source verification and client relevance |
| Cash exposure | Upfront supplier obligations and refunds | Tool, analyst and service fulfillment costs |
| Founder time | Sales, deal coordination, vendor management | Sales, qualification, briefing and customer support |
| True profit | Receipts – direct delivery – overhead | Collected fees – direct research – overhead |
| Transferable asset | Repeatable delivery economics & partner processes | Retained subscribers, SOPs, permitted datasets |
| Exit if weak | Decline capital-intensive projects | Stop low-value lead-list subscriptions |

## Resource allocation
Until there is evidence, put **most operating effort into live validation**, not high-cost agents. Continue government and AI venture monitoring at research priority, but prioritize private digital customers and research clients for revenue tests.

## Commercial options after validation
1. Keep Track A as a selective opportunity capture tool.
2. Scale Track B as a specialist fixed-fee research operation.
3. License the financial screening and supplier workflow as an embedded module.
4. Explore white-label only after secure multi-tenant infrastructure, actual demand and maintenance capacity.

**Never merge Track A project revenue with Track B recurring fees when presenting a SaaS valuation.**
