# WATCHTOWER AI | 90-day operating proof plan
**Baseline date:** November 17, 2026. **Status:** PLANNED; no outcomes or customer interest are assumed.
**Owner:** MLive Digital Solutions LLC. This document is a public process plan, not the place for private buyer, vendor, financial, or contact records.

## Mission
In 90 days, establish whether Watchtower can (a) find real, affordable outsourced business engagements and/or (b) provide research sufficiently useful that businesses will pay for an intelligence briefing. A third track, improving the technology, is constrained to changes that enable those tests.

**Success is evidence, not the number of dashboard features.**

## Non-negotiable constraints
- Maximum $1,000 conservative peak cash exposure **per deal and across active obligations**.
- Minimum 25% projected contribution after vendor costs, direct expenses and reserves.
- $2,500/month realized contribution is an **aspiration, not a Day-90 commitment**.
- Aim for 1–2 owner hours/week once mature; record the higher, actual time spent during testing.
- All vendor outreach, customer proposals, spending and contracts require specific owner authorization.
- Public code and documents: **no customer-sensitive data or supplier quotations** in GitHub Pages or this repository.

## Days 1–14 | Instrument before outreach
**Deliverables**
- [ ] Select the first narrow digital-service niche (for example, fixed-scope CRM cleanup or process automation) and define acceptable contract sizes and vendor skill criteria.
- [ ] Set up a private research ledger outside the public repo (private credentials and contract details must not enter this static demo).
- [ ] Begin logging owner time by task category: discovery, source checks, supplier research, sales, proposals, delivery oversight, collections and product development.
- [ ] Create the first 5–10 sourced opportunity candidates and record why each passed or failed preliminary fit.
- [ ] Design one **fixed-fee intelligence pilot offer** to test, with defined scope, delivery schedule and excluded guarantees.
- [ ] Identify possible research-pilot clients as hypotheses; no claims of interest until conversations occur.
- [ ] Decide whether to configure the optional SAM.gov collector. Check source terms, retention, deadlines and publisher rights before doing so.
- [ ] Verify chain-of-title work items; decide what must remain in a private repository.

**No-go signal:** lead sources are stale/unreliable, or collecting even five real candidates is more laborious than expected.

## Days 15–30 | Run two tests without confusing them
**Track A — outsourced digital service deal**
- [ ] Review ten actual, date-stamped buyer requests (cumulative).
- [ ] Select up to three for deeper scope/cash analysis and identify multiple plausible suppliers.
- [ ] Record precisely why each candidate is eligible, low-touch, and funded.

**Track B — research/intelligence service**
- [ ] Reach out to up to five potential pilot customers **only after owner approval**.
- [ ] Test willingness to pay for source-backed, curated opportunities with explicit scope and limits.
- [ ] Record real objections, delivery hours, renewal concerns, price feedback and next steps.
- [ ] Do not convert expressed curiosity into a recorded customer or paid subscription.

**Decision:** which track has demonstrated credible demand *per owner hour*? Continue the stronger track; pause optional software polish.

## Days 31–60 | Validate cost, delivery and price
- [ ] Reach 20 real sourced leads (cumulative), including rejected leads and evidence.
- [ ] Complete five concise diligence dossiers with original source references and unknowns.
- [ ] Obtain at least two actual written subcontractor quotes where legally and contractually appropriate.
- [ ] Quantify contribution **after direct costs and contingency**, not gross client revenue.
- [ ] Model worst-case cash timing and the $1,000 active portfolio cap.
- [ ] Seek a paid or explicitly declined fixed-fee research pilot offer; record the outcome.
- [ ] Calculate real elapsed owner time/lead, time/verified deal, time/vendor response and time/client conversation.
- [ ] Produce one explicit no-go decision triggered by actual evidence, if the evidence warrants it; do not manufacture a rejection for the metric.

**No-go signal:** no buyer demand, supplier quotes make margins unattractive, or onboarding/research overhead defeats the low-touch objective.

## Days 61–90 | Prove one real transaction or document why not
- [ ] If a compliant, financially suitable candidate exists, prepare a proposal **for separate owner approval**.
- [ ] If a research service client agrees, deliver a bounded pilot and collect feedback.
- [ ] Record actual income **only when received**, direct expenses when incurred, refund/collection exposure and owner hours.
- [ ] If a deal closes, collect signed scope, terms and acceptance evidence in **private storage**, not this repo.
- [ ] Update a truthful commercial evidence summary: counts, actual revenue, contribution, time, source reliability and limitations.
- [ ] Hold a go/narrow/pivot/stop review for each revenue track.
- [ ] Reprioritize product development only after reviewing these results.

## Evidence-based decision criteria
| Question | Evidence | Action if weak |
|---|---|---|
| Does demand exist? | Original buyer request or signed paid research pilot | Narrow source/segment |
| Can someone else deliver? | Verified partner capacity + written quote + scope | Build supplier bench or pass |
| Will it fund safely? | Payment schedule, liabilities and peak cash gap | Change terms or decline |
| Does it earn worthwhile contribution? | Real receipts minus direct expenses/reserves | Stop low-margin categories |
| Is owner effort tolerable? | Logged hours, including follow-up and collection | Delegate with realistic budget or stop |
| Can an acquirer replicate it? | Measurable outcomes, owned IP, workflows, rights | Do not assume valuation premium |

## Weekly founder review (suggested: 20–30 minutes, plus required actions)
Review: 5 highest-confidence items, 5 biggest blockers, actual founder hours, cash currently at risk, unpaid invoices, experiments, and the next approval. A weekly review is not a claim that the entire business can currently run in 30 minutes.

## Rules for honest reporting
Never relabel demo scenarios as leads, unquoted suppliers as qualified partners, signed proposals as revenue, booked/contracted invoices as collected cash, or historical government spending as an open solicitation.
