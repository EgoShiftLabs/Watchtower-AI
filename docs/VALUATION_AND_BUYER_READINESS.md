# WATCHTOWER AI | Valuation discipline and transfer-readiness
**Status:** Methodology for future diligence. **Not a professional appraisal or price quote. No buyer interest or transaction value is claimed.**

## Separate three fundamentally different questions
### 1. Rebuild/replacement cost
What would it cost a qualified developer/team to recreate the *existing, tested and transferable* functionality? Cost is not automatically market price. Exclude nonfunctional plans from claimed asset scope, and compare the buyer's opportunity cost, speed advantage and maintenance burden.

### 2. Current software-asset acquisition price
What price would a specific buyer *actually be willing to pay* for this package, considering legal ownership, public code, competing alternatives, security, quality and transition effort? May be substantially below replacement cost. Without competing signed offers or verified comparable non-revenue asset transactions, use an **uncertain negotiating range**, not a valuation.

### 3. Operating-business purchase price
Once revenue exists, distinguish:
- Recurring software license receipts, renewals, retention, software gross margin and operating costs.
- Human-delivered research retainers and their service labor/retention.
- Managed digital project revenue, delivery obligations, dispute/refund rates and seller discretionary earnings.
Apply relevant transaction comparables only to the *matching revenue mix*. Never apply software ARR multiples to one-off subcontracted project billings.

## When valuation methods become defensible
- **Now:** inventory verified code/IP, test evidence, repository visibility, developer replacement estimates and documentation. No commercial price can be confidently inferred without offers.
- **After verified paid pilots:** actual paid demand and project profitability may support an evidence-based asset discussion.
- **After repeatable recurring receipts:** retention, gross margin, concentrated customer risk and founder dependence may support a market-comparable analysis.
- **After transferable operating infrastructure:** security, clean title, support documentation and rights to source data can reduce buyer risk.

## Asset-sale decision tree
1. Confirm legal ownership, contributors, AI-tool terms and assignment rights.
2. Confirm name/trademark availability, third-party dataset/API terms and contract transfer restrictions.
3. Separate public demo from private operational intelligence before storing data/quotes.
4. Establish whether a buyer wants an asset purchase, component license, white-label license, acquihire or business acquisition.
5. Record truly comparable **closed** transactions, including transaction date, revenue mix and buyer profile.
6. Estimate expected buyer rebuild cost and integration cost; model transfer/support obligations.
7. Collect actual expressions of interest **without misrepresenting traction**.
8. Have qualified counsel review definitive agreements, confidentiality, indemnity, escrow and assignment.

## Why the current project is not a multi-tenant AI platform
- Static GitHub Pages frontend and deterministic screening logic are implemented.
- A public SAM.gov collector exists in code but requires configuration and successful live validation.
- No secure backend, AI research inference runtime, paying users, supplier network, recurring customer base, revenue or proprietary dataset has been demonstrated.
- The current repo is public, so code uniqueness and trade-secret claims cannot be assumed.

## Buyer proof package: an order of operations
1. Technical artifact inventory and tests.
2. Source-data provenance, privacy plan and IP chain-of-title.
3. Evidence of the two revenue tests (actual buyer interest, pilot quote, money collected).
4. Reconciled financials, owner hours and retained obligations.
5. Repeated outcomes and known failure modes.
6. Buyer-specific rights, transfer costs, support and integration assumptions.

## Decision policy
Do not announce a sale price or build to an optimistic acquisition multiple. First prove that someone pays for either profitable business execution or evidence-backed opportunity intelligence. Evaluate an unsolicited offer on cash now, future royalties/licensing rights, exclusivity, transition burden, tax/legal cost, and founder lifestyle fit.
